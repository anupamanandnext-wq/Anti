<h2>Why Do Acne Scars Develop?</h2>

<p>Acne scars can form when inflammation from acne damages the deeper layers of the skin. As the skin heals, its natural repair process may produce too little or too much collagen in the affected area. This can result in depressed or raised scars.</p>

<p>Several factors may contribute to acne scarring:</p>

<ul>
<li>Picking, squeezing or scratching active pimples</li>
<li>Deep or inflamed acne</li>
<li>Delayed treatment of persistent acne</li>
<li>Repeated breakouts in the same area</li>
<li>Individual differences in the skin's healing process</li>
</ul>

<p>Not every acne mark is a permanent scar. Some marks are temporary changes in skin colour, such as dark spots or redness left after a breakout. A dermatologist can help distinguish between post-acne marks and actual scars before suggesting treatment.</p>

<h2>What Are the Different Types of Acne Scars?</h2>

<p>Identifying the type of scar is important because different scars may respond to different treatments.</p>

<h3>1. Ice Pick Scars</h3>
<p>These are narrow, deep scars that create small openings in the skin. They can extend into deeper layers and may require specialised treatment approaches.</p>

<h3>2. Boxcar Scars</h3>
<p>Boxcar scars usually appear as round or oval depressions with clearly defined edges. They can make the skin look uneven, particularly around the cheeks and temples.</p>

<h3>3. Rolling Scars</h3>
<p>Rolling scars create a wavy or uneven appearance on the skin. They develop when fibrous bands beneath the skin pull the surface downward.</p>

<h3>4. Raised Acne Scars</h3>
<p>Some people develop raised scars when excess scar tissue forms during healing. These may require a different treatment approach from depressed acne scars.</p>

<h2>Which Treatments May Help Improve Acne Scars?</h2>

<p>There is no single treatment that works equally well for every type of acne scar. A dermatologist considers the scar pattern, skin type, acne activity and previous treatments before recommending an approach.</p>

<h3>Chemical Peel Treatment</h3>
<p>Chemical peels use selected skin-care solutions to exfoliate the outer layers of the skin. Depending on the formulation and depth, they may help improve certain superficial acne marks, uneven tone and skin texture. The type of peel and its suitability should be determined by a qualified professional.</p>

<h3>Laser Skin Treatment</h3>
<p>Certain laser treatments may be considered to improve the appearance of acne scars by targeting specific skin layers and supporting skin remodelling. The choice of laser depends on the scar type, skin tone and individual risk factors.</p>

<h3>Microneedling</h3>
<p>Microneedling uses fine needles to create controlled micro-injuries in the skin. This process stimulates the skin's natural repair response and may help improve selected types of acne scarring over a series of sessions.</p>

<h3>Subcision</h3>
<p>Subcision may be considered for certain depressed or rolling scars. It involves releasing fibrous bands beneath the skin that pull the surface downward. A dermatologist must assess whether this procedure is appropriate.</p>

<p>The expected response, number of sessions and recovery period can vary. A combination of approaches may sometimes be considered, depending on the individual's skin condition.</p>
